From watching to making · Product walkthrough

A tutorial becomes
something you can do.

Follow Jamie from a saved playlist to a landing-page draft. These are real screenshots of the interactive concept, with the learner’s next action and the product’s current limits explained at every step.

Try the repaired interactive prototype ↗ · Read the PRD ↗

For whom

Early-career professionals and career switchers building their first portfolio artifact from a YouTube tutorial.

First use case

A responsive landing page, finished in one to three weeks with 2–6 hours of practice per week.

The value to test

A clear next exercise, evidence of application, and feedback that leads to another iteration.

SCREEN 01 / 07

Start with an outcome.

Jamie arrives with a saved tutorial and a project in mind. The overview makes the next action visible, alongside the current path and practice progress. The returning-learner sample starts at 2 of 5 completed tasks.

The action: Choose Turn a playlist into a plan to start a fresh path, or continue a lesson.

Prototype boundary: A production version would use the learner’s actual paths. The current sample contains one fictional learner and one authored curriculum.

SCREEN 02 / 07

Give the playlist a purpose.

Jamie pastes a playlist, names the thing they want to make, chooses a weekly time budget, and sets a starting level. A goal such as “build a landing page” makes the end state concrete.

The action: Select Use the sample playlist instead, then Create my sample plan. An invalid host produces an inline error and leaves the form recoverable.

Prototype boundary: The prototype validates the URL and loads a fixed fixture. Real metadata retrieval, source selection, and generated exercises are future requirements.

SCREEN 03 / 07

See the route to a finished project.

The plan connects four short study sessions to two practice phases and one final project. Jamie sees the estimated commitment before beginning, rather than treating the playlist as an endless queue.

The action: Open a lesson. Review the project brief at any point. Four hours a week gives a two-week estimate for the six-hour budget.

Prototype boundary: Changing weekly hours adjusts the time estimate only. Real prerequisite checks, adaptive sequencing, and editable tasks remain unbuilt.

SCREEN 04 / 07

Watch, capture, then apply.

Jamie opens the original creator video, saves a note in their own words, and sees a task beside it. The saved note shown here explains how the layout will change on mobile.

The action: Mark the source watched, switch between notes and practice prompts, then open the task checklist. Notes and watched status survive reload.

Prototype boundary: The video panel is an external-source launcher, not an embedded player. Prompts are authored examples, not transcript-grounded AI output. Watching does not complete a task.

SCREEN 05 / 07

Make progress by making something.

Jamie works through five small exercises that contribute to the final page. Three are checked in this screenshot, so practice progress is 60%; two remain open.

The action: Check or undo a task, filter by To do or Completed, and return to its lesson when stuck.

Prototype boundary: Checks record self-assessment. A real pilot should collect an artifact or short explanation and distinguish attempted, blocked, and demonstrated skill.

SCREEN 06 / 07

Submit a draft, not a claim of mastery.

Jamie shares a project link and a reflection about the layout decisions, then names what to improve. The rubric makes the intended evaluation dimensions visible before submission.

The action: Enter a URL and at least 30 characters of reflection. A draft is allowed before every task is complete.

Prototype boundary: The example.com URL is an illustrative placeholder. Submission saves a draft locally; no site is visited, uploaded, or evaluated.

SCREEN 07 / 07

Leave with the next iteration.

The draft receives three specific prompts: test the headline, check the mobile layout, and explain the tradeoff. The learner has a concrete reason to return to the work.

The action: Update the draft, make one change, or Download project summary to keep a Markdown artifact.

Prototype boundary: Feedback is explicitly simulated and deterministic. Future assessment needs evidence, permission, a validated rubric, and a clear “cannot evaluate” state. No score is assigned here.

The same learning loop, on smaller screens

Review on your phone. Build where you work best.

Phone navigation scrolls horizontally. Content columns stack so notes and tasks stay usable. Coding the final artifact can happen in a separate tool on a larger screen.
